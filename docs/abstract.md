# Abstract

Results: 2030, cumulative vs 2025, run with `--output-includes-power-passthrough --it-utilisation 0.7` (see methodology.md, section 6).

Operational datacenters are concentrated in the United States (5,044), United Kingdom (595), Germany (580), China (443), France (401), India (338), Australia (337) and Canada (299). By 2030, capacity could grow from 62.2 to 151.7 GW in the United States (S&P Global, 2026), from 3 to 5 GW in Germany (Bitkom, 2026), from 32 to over 60 GW in China (Rystad Energy, 2026) and from 1.6 to 3.9 GW in Australia (Data Centres Australia, 2026). UK grid requests total 50 GW (Savills, 2026), and France leverages nuclear power for 3.1 GW of SoftBank capacity (Reuters, 2026). Canada (cool climate, land, water, renewables) ranks eighth in installed and second in planned capacity (22.2 GW), reaching 23.6 GW by 2037 (Statista, 2026; Carlo et al., 2026). India (digital economy, AI investment) ranks fifth in installed and third in planned capacity (8.65 GW), growing from 1.57 GW in 2026 to 8 GW by 2030 (Knight Frank India, 2026; Press Information Bureau, 2026a). Although Canada's grid is largely non-emitting, 92% of planned capacity is in Alberta, where behind-the-meter gas is not covered by the Clean Electricity Regulations (Beugin and Harland, 2026); India relies on coal. AI could generate 131–224.8 kt of e-waste annually by 2030 (de Vries-Gao, 2026).
Our environmentally extended input-output framework combines the OECD's 2025 Inter-Country Input-Output tables, Air Emission Accounts and SEEA-harmonised GTAP 12 emissions. Digital supply and use tables separate cloud computing and hosting within Canada's NAICS 51821 and datacenters within India's NIC 6311 (OECD, 2023). We project to 2030 using OECD methods (OECD, 2026), E3MC, IEO, AEO, national electricity projections and 70% utilisation (Shehabi et al., 2024). We separate direct datacenter emissions, upstream emissions embodied in inputs and electricity, and downstream emissions in goods and services produced with datacenter services.
We apply the framework to all eight countries, reporting Canada and India as examples. Over 2025–2030, datacenter output rises by USD 4.83 billion (Canada) and 8.41 billion (India), raising global output by USD 7.58 and 25.32 billion and domestic value added by 4.34 and 5.56 billion. Despite this short-term output gain, the environmental impacts of datacenter expansion are dire. 2030 emissions rise by 8.04 and 36.99 MtCO2e, 1.15% and 0.90% of 2022 national emissions. If behind-the-meter gas (0.356 kg CO2e/kWh) supplies 92% of Canadian electricity, direct emissions reach 7.67 MtCO2e (6.84 MtCO2e above the projected 0.038 kg CO2e/kWh grid) and upstream 0.37 MtCO2e. India's emissions are mostly upstream (36.90 MtCO2e, 77% from grid electricity; 0.08 MtCO2e direct). Downstream emissions add 1.25 and 13.17 MtCO2e. Five foreign suppliers, led by the United States and China, produce USD 656 million of Canada's supply chain (0.11 MtCO2e), mainly servers and IT services; five led by China produce USD 2.36 billion of India's (0.63 MtCO2e), mainly electronics, coal, oil and gas. Cooling water could reach 5.3 and 42.8 billion litres, and e-waste 44.8 and 82.8 kt, by 2030. Our findings complicate net-zero and renewable objectives (SDGs 7 and 13), intensify water stress in India (SDG 6), and undermine responsible consumption and production (SDG 12).
